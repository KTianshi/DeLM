Constructive maximum matching and Gallai-Edmonds decomposition

Let G=(V,E) be an arbitrary finite undirected simple graph, with vertices
numbered 1,...,n. A matching is a subset of E whose edges have pairwise disjoint
endpoints. Write nu(G) for the maximum cardinality of a matching in G. A perfect
matching covers every vertex. For X contained in V, G[X] is the induced graph
on X and G-X is the induced graph on V minus X. Let o(H) be the number of
connected components of H having an odd number of vertices. A graph H is
factor-critical if H-v has a perfect matching for every vertex v of H.
The empty graph has a perfect matching of size zero.

Give one self-contained constructive treatment of maximum matching in general
graphs that establishes ALL the following results.

1. Maximum matching algorithm.
Give a deterministic polynomial-time blossom-contraction algorithm returning a
maximum-cardinality matching M of G. Define the search objects, their invariants,
the circumstances in which contraction is allowed, how contracted edges retain
original endpoint information, and how a matching or augmenting path is lifted
when contractions are undone. Include precise mathematical pseudocode or an
equally precise algorithm description. Prove the augmenting-path criterion and
all contraction, lifting, progress and termination claims on which your algorithm
relies. Handle nested contractions explicitly; naming Edmonds' algorithm or
assuming a contraction lemma is not sufficient.

2. Optimality certificate and min-max theorem.
Give a polynomial-time procedure returning U contained in V together with M
such that
  2|M| = n + |U| - o(G-U).
Prove the universal upper bound for every matching and every U, and thereby prove
  nu(G) = min over U contained in V of (n + |U| - o(G-U))/2.
The procedure must construct U, not only prove its existence or search all subsets.

3. Gallai-Edmonds structural theorem and its construction.
Define
  D = {v in V : some maximum matching of G leaves v uncovered},
  A = {v in V minus D : v has a neighbor in D},
  C = V minus (D union A).
Prove that each connected component of G[D] is factor-critical and that G[C]
has a perfect matching. Prove that every maximum matching consists of a perfect
matching on C, a matching covering all but one vertex within each component of
G[D], and edges matching every vertex of A to vertices in distinct components
of G[D]. Prove conversely that every matching of this described form is maximum,
and prove that U=A achieves the certificate equality in part 2.
Give a polynomial-time procedure computing D,A,C. Reusing your already proved
maximum matching algorithm is allowed, but account for every invocation.

4. Full algorithmic accounting and edge cases.
Give explicit polynomial upper bounds for all procedures above, including search,
contraction, lifting, certificate construction and decomposition. An optimal
exponent is not required. The input is an n-by-n adjacency matrix; you may use
a word RAM with O(log(n+2))-bit words. State your representations and account for
their manipulation, including parallel edges or endpoint records introduced by
contraction. Address n=0, isolated vertices, disconnected graphs, graphs with
perfect matchings, and components consisting of a single vertex.

Prove every matching-specific theorem or lemma that you use. In particular,
Berge's lemma, Tutte's theorem, the Tutte-Berge formula, blossom contraction
results, Hall's theorem, and the Gallai-Edmonds theorem may not be invoked as
unproved black boxes. Elementary finite-set arguments, induction and standard
graph traversal algorithms may be used without proof. A named theorem, a
reference to a published solution, or computational testing does not replace a
proof. Definitions introduced in one part must remain consistent in all parts.

The deliverable is one complete mathematical proof with precise algorithm
descriptions, not an executable implementation. Establishing only the matching
algorithm or only the min-max formula does not satisfy the task.
