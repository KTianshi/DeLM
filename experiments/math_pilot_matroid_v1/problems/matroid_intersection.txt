Constructive matroid intersection

Let E be a finite set, with n = |E|. A matroid on E is a family I of subsets
of E satisfying these three axioms:
(1) The empty set belongs to I.
(2) If A belongs to I and B is a subset of A, then B belongs to I.
(3) If A and B belong to I and |A| < |B|, there is an element b in B minus A
    such that A union {b} belongs to I.
Members of I are called independent sets. Its rank function is
r(X) = max{|J| : J is a subset of X and J belongs to I} for X a subset of E.

You are given two matroids M1 = (E, I1) and M2 = (E, I2). Access to each
matroid is through an independence oracle: for a submitted subset X of E,
the oracle reports whether X belongs to its family of independent sets.
The families are not supplied as explicit lists. Let r1 and r2 be their
rank functions.

Prove the following constructive theorem for every such pair of finite matroids:
There is a deterministic algorithm, using a number of oracle queries and
additional elementary operations polynomial in n, that returns sets S and
U contained in E such that
  S belongs to both I1 and I2, and
  |S| = r1(U) + r2(E minus U).

In particular, prove the min-max identity
  max{|J| : J belongs to both I1 and I2}
    = min{r1(X) + r2(E minus X) : X is a subset of E}.

Give a precise mathematical description or pseudocode for the algorithm,
a complete proof of its correctness and termination, and explicit polynomial
bounds on its oracle queries and additional work. An optimal exponent is not
required. Count each oracle query as one operation; represent subsets of E
by length-n bit vectors and account for their manipulation when bounding
additional work. Address the empty ground set and matroids of rank zero.

The proof must be self-contained starting from the three matroid axioms above.
Prove every additional matroid property or lemma you use. Elementary finite-set
arguments and standard graph traversal algorithms may be used without proving
them. Do not invoke matroid intersection, matroid union, or another optimization
or min-max theorem as an unproved black box. Merely naming a known algorithm
or citing a published proof does not satisfy the task.

The deliverable is a mathematical proof with an algorithm description; an
executable implementation is not required. Finite computational tests cannot
replace the general proof.
